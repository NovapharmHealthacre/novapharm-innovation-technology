Destination-market lenses

Different markets create different development questions.

The examples below describe planning themes. The final route is confirmed project by project with current guidance and the relevant qualified specialists.

United Kingdom

UK-oriented development planning

Consider the intended MHRA route, applicant and licence-holder structure, dossier expectations, manufacturing arrangements, labelling implications and post-approval responsibilities.

European Union

EU procedure and partner mapping

Frame the likely national or European pathway, reference-product questions, member-state strategy, dossier implications and roles of EU-established regulated parties.

Canada / United States

North American gap assessment

Identify product-specific evidence, comparative-development, facility, filing and local specialist questions before selecting a formal pathway or development commitment.

India

India development and commercial interface

Connect CDSCO-related questions, manufacturing strategy, local product classification, evidence requirements and commercialisation responsibilities with the appointed parties.

MENA

Country-specific partner planning

Treat MENA as multiple jurisdiction-specific pathways rather than one market, with local registration, representation, legalisation and commercial requirements assessed separately.

Australia / other markets

Specialist-led market expansion

Use an evidence-led screening step to determine whether the product, existing approvals and documentation support a credible discussion with an appropriate local adviser.

Decision framework

Four questions before choosing a destination market.

Is the product defined?

Dosage form, strength, use, presentation and differentiation must be explicit.

Is the evidence portable?

Existing data may not meet the selected market, route or product-specific expectation.

Is the supply chain ready?

Manufacturing, testing, release, importation and distribution roles must align.

Is ownership clear?

The applicant, licence holder, local representative and commercial owner need defined accountability.

Is the economics credible?

Development cost, timing, pricing, demand and partner terms must support the decision.
